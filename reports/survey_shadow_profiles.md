# Shadow profiles: the literature behind the inferred / elicited split

Ahnaf An Nafee · 23 September 2026

Scope: the shadow profile as defined in `reports/shadow_profile_design.md` — any user representation the system assembles beyond the history deliberately attached to the request. Two acquisition modes are kept apart throughout: **inferred / already held** (records the platform has and currently discards, free to the user) and **elicited** (answers bought with questions). **Cross-surface** means the same person's activity on another part of the catalogue. Verification method: every source below is a paper page on `arxiv.org`, opened from this session; titles, venues and author lists were taken from the arXiv listing itself (`arxiv.org/abs/...` and the `export.arxiv.org` metadata API) on 23 September 2026. Where a page does not state a venue, the entry says so rather than guessing. Claims below are weakened to what the abstract on the opened page supports.

Relevance is gated by three constraints of this laboratory. It ranks a full catalogue with one positive target per request and scores NDCG@10 only. It has no exposure log, no propensities, no user study. Its archives hold `user_id`, `parent_asin`, rating, timestamp and a history string — no item attributes, no graph.

## What this repository does

The committed route lives in `reliability/core.py` and `reliability/benchmark.py`. `last_distinct(items, limit=20)` takes the profile. `top_neighbors(user_positives, limit=20, neighbors_per_item=50)` builds a cosine co-review similarity from training users. `Recommender.train(positive_events, catalog, cutoff_ms, recent_days=365)` fits a lifetime and a recent popularity base; `Recommender.rank_grid` blends them as `(1 - alpha) * base + alpha * personalized` and takes an exact top-k. `ndcg_one_target(ranking, target)` is `1 / log2(rank + 2)` on a single target. Split boundaries `T1`, `T2`, the blend grid `ALPHAS`, `training_data`, `quality` and `user_cluster_interval` come from `benchmark.py`; `validation_decision.json` is written before the test archive is opened, and a challenger wins only if the paired user-cluster bootstrap's lower 95% endpoint exceeds zero. On the test period the committed hybrid scores 0.008681 against validation-selected recent popularity at 0.008007 over 35,905 eligible positive-review requests, a paired difference of +0.000674 [0.000040, 0.001338].

The shadow arms live in `reliability/shadow.py`. Constants fix the vocabulary: `NEGATIVE_RATING = 3`, `POSITIVE_RATING = 4`, `RATING_WEIGHT = {5: 1.0, 4: 0.6}`, `GAMMAS = (0.0, 0.25, 0.5, 1.0)`, `HALF_LIVES = (None, 1095, 365, 90)`, `QUESTION_BUDGETS = (0, 3, 10, 30, 100)`, `INTERVIEW_DEPTH = 200`, plus the history-size `BUCKET_BOUNDS`. `ShadowRecommender.evidence` splits a history into attraction and repulsion using only the records the platform holds; an item with no held rating stays a co-review signal with no inferred sentiment. `novice_prior(positives, catalog)` builds a log1p-normalised aggregate first-positive-review distribution for requests with nothing personalisable. `interview_order(positives, depth=INTERVIEW_DEPTH)` asks about the most often positively reviewed catalogue items, and `ShadowRecommender.interview` never asks about an item in the supplied history or the current target, charging a question even when the user has nothing to say. `adjacent_projection(donor_positives, target_positives, depth=50)` projects a donor category's positives through overlapping users with 1/fan weighting. `ShadowRecommender.rank` widens the base slice by exactly the number of depressed candidates so that repulsion cannot smuggle a candidate past the exact top-k argument. `tools/signal_audit.py` publishes aggregate counts only — `history_slots_rating_known`, `history_slots_rating_below_four`, `history_slots_without_user_item_training_rating`, `history_slots_item_absent_from_train_catalog`, `distinct_items_in_profile_cap` and its `_beyond` counterpart, `first_seen_user`, `history_column_exact_matches` — against `PROFILE_CAP = 20`.

The text side is in `reports/phrase_to_products.md`: a `TextRanker` with `scope = "behavioural"` and `scope_depth = 2000`, `HELD_SENTENCES = 8`, six phrase conditions, and the measured result that a phrase taken at catalogue scope *lost* 0.0034 NDCG@10 across 400 requests.

## Literature, grouped by the decision it informs

### 1. Whether records already held say anything about preferences

[SINE: Understanding and Modeling Passive-Negative Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.04086) (Pan et al., RecSys 2023) analyses a short-video log where playback is automatic and skipping is the only refusal available, and trains a sub-interest encoder on positive *and* passive-negative feedback. Taken from this: a refusal to engage is a supervision signal about the user's current interest, not missing data.

[Learning from Negative User Feedback and Measuring Responsiveness](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12256) (Wang et al., RecSys 2023 Industry) adds explicit and implicit negatives to a retrieval objective with a "not-to-recommend" loss and measures responsiveness with counterfactual simulation on a large industrial system. Taken from this: negative evidence belongs in the objective at the retrieval stage, and a system that consumes it needs a metric that can see the response.

[Recommendations as Treatments](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.05352) (Schnabel et al., ICML 2016) states that observation is caused by user self-selection *and* by the system's own past output, and supplies inverse-propensity estimators plus a debiased factorisation. Taken from this: an inferred profile is a record of an observation process, so both its training and its evaluation need the propensity treated explicitly.

### 2. What to put in place of a missing profile

[Cold-Start Recommendation towards the Era of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01945) (Zhang et al., 2025) maps the field's development path from content features to graph relations to domain information to model world knowledge. Taken from this: cold-start methods are ordered by what conditioning variable they are allowed to read, and a method with none is not a weak version of the others — it is a different method.

[A Semi-Personalized System for User Cold Start Recommendation on Music Streaming Apps](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03819) (Briand et al., KDD 2021) clusters users from heterogeneous sources on Deezer, serves new users from the cluster rather than the individual, and reports offline and online validation of a deployed system. Taken from this: the working cold-start shape is semi-personalized — the profile is a group assignment, and it is judged online.

[Latent Contextual Bandits and their Application to Personalized Recommendations for New Users](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.06743) (Zhou and Brunskill, IJCAI 2016) infers latent user classes from prior users and uses them to condition bandit decisions for new users, with an improved regret bound and real-data validation. Taken from this: latent classes beat feature-only conditioning, and the currency for this arm is regret over a sequence, not one-shot ranking accuracy.

[Combating the Cold Start User Problem in Model Based Collaborative Filtering](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00397) (Biswas, Lakshmanan and Basu Ray, arXiv 2017, no venue stated on the page) formalises which items to show a new user as an optimisation problem and shows its computational cost. Taken from this: the choice of what to ask is itself a decision with an objective, not a fixed onboarding script.

[Budget-Constrained Item Cold-Start Handling in Collaborative Filtering Recommenders via Optimal Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2431) (Anava et al., arXiv v3 2016, no venue stated on the page) proves the selection objective monotone-supermodular and solves it under a budget with a Netflix study. Taken from this: with a limited number of observations to buy, greedy selection has a guarantee and a fixed order does not.

[A Re-visit of the Popularity Baseline in Recommender Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.13829) (Ji et al., SIGIR 2020) shows a popularity baseline scored at the moment of interaction improves by 70% or more over one counted from training data, and reports that low-activity users follow the crowd while heavy users follow their own preferences. Taken from this: a popularity control frozen at split time understates itself, and the crowd is the correct baseline specifically for the least-experienced users.

### 3. Side information the archive would have to hold

[Knowledge Graph Convolutional Networks for Recommender Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.12575) (Wang et al., WWW 2019) mines inter-item relatedness through attributes linked on a graph and aggregates over configurable hops. Taken from this: side information pays when items are connected by shared attributes, which is a property of the data, not of the model.

[KGAT: Knowledge Graph Attention Network for Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07854) (Wang et al., KDD 2019) propagates high-order connectivities — two items linked by one or more shared attributes — end to end in the ranking model. Taken from this: the enrichment is injected into the ranking function itself rather than into a separate user model, which is the same seam this repository uses for its blend.

[BLaIR: Benchmarking LLMs as Semantic Encoders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.03952) (Hou et al., v2 2026, ACL 2026 per the listing) benchmarks language models as encoders over both language and items and reports that their rankings here correlate little with general text-embedding rankings. Taken from this: text that works for retrieval in general does not transfer for free to catalogue ranking, so a text-based profile must be measured on the catalogue.

### 4. Expanding a profile with implicit feedback from more history or another surface

[Search-based User Interest Modeling with Lifelong Sequential Behavior Data](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.05639) (Qi et al., 2020) handles lifelong sequences — lengths up to 54,000 — by retrieving a target-relevant subsequence first and only then feeding it to the ranking model, deployed at Alibaba. Taken from this: a long history is useful only after a retrieval stage selects the part relevant to the current candidate.

[TWIN: TWo-stage Interest Network for Lifelong User Behavior Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02352) (Chang et al., KDD 2023) fixes the inconsistency between the search stage's relevance metric and the ranking stage's, over behaviour sequences of 10^4 to 10^5 at Kuaishou. Taken from this: the gains from long histories depend on the two stages agreeing about what "relevant" means, which is a checkable property of a pipeline.

[A Survey on Cross-domain Recommendation: Taxonomies, Methods, and Future Directions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.03357) (Zang et al., 2021, v2 2022) organises the field around using activity in other domains against sparsity and cold start. Taken from this: cross-surface transfer is the standard answer to "this user has nothing here", and it is normally evaluated where overlap between the two logs is substantial.

[One for All, All for One: Learning and Transferring User Embeddings for Cross-Domain Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.11964) (Li et al., WSDM 2023) trains shared user embeddings across domains and reports that transferring them can hurt the target domain, with the risk rising when a domain is sparse. Taken from this: negative transfer is an expected outcome of borrowing a profile, not an anomaly, so a cross-surface arm needs to be able to detect that it lost.

[User Identity Linkage on Social Networks: A Review of Modern Techniques and Applications](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.08966) (Senette, Siino and Tesconi, IEEE Access 2024) reviews formulations, features, algorithms, datasets and metrics for deciding that two accounts are one person. Taken from this: matching an identity across surfaces is a measured step with an error rate, and that rate bounds whatever is built on the join.

### 5. Asking, and being told: elicited versus volunteered

[A Survey on Conversational Recommender Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.00646) (Jannach, Manzoor, Cai and Chen, ACM Computing Surveys 54(5), 2021) reviews systems that elicit preferences through questions and let users question the recommendations, and discusses how the field evaluates them. Taken from this: elicitation is a distinct interaction with its own evaluation practice, generally including participant experience alongside hit metrics.

[Generating Usage-related Questions for Preference Elicitation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.13463) (Kostric, Balog and Radlinski, ACM ToRS 2(2), 2024) generates questions about how an item was used rather than about its attributes, and evaluates the questions with both automatic and human rating. Taken from this: question content is the object being designed, and its quality is measured separately from recommendation accuracy.

[Preference Elicitation with Soft Attributes in Interactive Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02085) (Biyik et al., 2023) unifies item-based and attribute-based elicitation using concept activation vectors, so a user may answer in vague self-described terms. Taken from this: item questions and attribute questions are two points in one design space, and a system that only asks about items occupies a small part of it.

[When and How to Ask: Dynamic Preference Elicitation Strategies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.06765) (Xia, Zhang and Wang, SIGIR 2026) shows empirically that the best strategy is stage-dependent: attribute questions help early, item questions later as preferences refine. Taken from this: a single fixed question type is the wrong policy for a whole session, and the earliest turn is where an item-only policy is weakest.

[A First Look at Selection Bias in Preference Elicitation for Recommendation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00554) (Gupta, Oosterhuis and de Rijke, CONSEQUENCES'23 at RecSys 2023) studies elicitation where which questions a user can answer is itself non-random, and shows that ignoring it early compounds into over-representation later. Taken from this: elicited answers arrive biased by what the user happens to have touched, and the bias propagates into subsequent recommendations.

[Minimizing Live Experiments in Recommender Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17436) (Hsu et al., RecSys 2024, per the DOI linked from the page) evaluates onboarding elicitation policies on YouTube Music with counterfactually robust simulated users because onboarding happens once per person and live tests are expensive. Taken from this: simulated respondents are a legitimate instrument for *comparing* elicitation policies, adopted precisely because they cannot replace the live measurement of cost.

[Open Bandit Dataset and Pipeline](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.07146) (Saito, Aihara, Matsutani and Narita, NeurIPS 2021 Datasets and Benchmarks) publishes logged bandit feedback from ZOZOTOWN with a standardised off-policy evaluation pipeline. Taken from this: when live experiments are unavailable, the honest instrument is off-policy evaluation on logged feedback with the logging policy recorded.

[Interactive Recommendation Agent with Active User Commands](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.21317) (Tang et al., 2025, listed as under review) puts natural-language commands inside a mainstream recommendation feed and handles them with a dual-agent Parser/Planner. Taken from this: text can arrive volunteered, in the feed, without any question being asked, and current systems route it through an interpretive step before it touches ranking.

## Open questions

1. Repulsion is scored with a metric that cannot see it. A responsiveness measure on the served slice — how often items similar to a below-four history item survive into the top-10 — is the measurement the 2023 industry papers use and this repository lacks; `ShadowRecommender.rank` already knows which candidates it depressed.
2. Whether γ = 1 is the boundary of a rising curve or its edge is unknown, and the question is cheap: extend `GAMMAS` past 1.0 and report the whole grid as C2 already does.
3. Does a retrieval stage make a wider profile pay? `top_neighbors` with `neighbors_per_item=50` is one hop from the SIM shape: select the history items relevant to the candidate before blending, then compare `PROFILE_CAP` 20 against the archive's full record for the 23% of items currently outside the window. The two changes must be tested together, since stage consistency is where TWIN locates the gain.
4. Is the 20+ bucket's preference for α = 0.5 a property of these categories or of a popularity base frozen at split time? Ji et al. predict an understated frozen baseline; a moment-of-interaction control would say whether the finding survives a stronger crowd, and it changes every challenger in this lab at once.
5. Would a conditioning variable that conditions help C3? A latent-class or cluster prior in the Deezer and latent-bandit sense requires something beyond category membership, and this archive offers co-review neighbourhood structure as the only available candidate.
6. What does an information-gain ordering buy over `interview_order`'s popularity ordering, at a fixed budget? Optimal design gives a greedy procedure and a guarantee; the measured 440-prompts-per-recorded-answer figure describes archive coverage under this fixed question order.
7. Do attribute questions beat item questions on the 1,288 empty-history requests specifically? The archive has no attributes, so the test needs a proxy — category and price-band questions against the existing item questions at budget 3.
8. Volunteered text inside the ranking loop is measured once, at catalogue scope, and lost. RecBot's shape is a command interpreted against the current request; whether an interpreted narrow phrase beats the same words read as a profile is unmeasured here.
9. Both acquisition modes are evaluated as if the scorer were the logging policy. Off-policy machinery is the instrument the field uses when live traffic is unavailable, and this repository has timestamps and a frozen split but no recorded logging policy.
10. No quantity here involves a person. The elicited column is questions asked, and the inferred side has no measurement of whether an assembled profile is noticeable or welcome; nothing in the sources above closes that gap with archive statistics.
